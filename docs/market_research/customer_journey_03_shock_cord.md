## 1. Market Demand (with evidence)

### 1.1 Who buys tent shock cord

- `FACT` **Tent owners who need to re-cord their own poles.** The dominant use case in every guide I fetched is the home repair of the customer's *own existing* tent. Kelty's support article is addressed to "How do I repair the shock cord at the center of my pole?" — the owner is the buyer. (https://help.kelty.com/support/solutions/articles/9000191715-how-do-i-repair-the-shock-cord-at-the-center-of-my-pole-)
- `FACT` **Tent repairers / paid service operators.** Sylvan Sport explicitly routes field-repairable breakage away from DIY to "many REI stores can do basic repairs. Another great option is TentPole Technologies, the nation's leading pole repair service." (https://support.sylvansport.com/how-to-fix-a-tent-pole) — this is a *commercial* buyer segment, not a DTC consumer.
- `FACT` **Ultralight / MYOG (make-your-own-gear) builders.** The BackpackingLight thread "[recycled dome to tarp – cording the poles]" shows a hobbyist converting a dome tent into a tarp and had to re-cord from scratch; he wrote *"I have always enjoyed MYOG … I have forgotten how to shock cord my poles."* (https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/20736/) This is a small, enthusiast, *build-once* segment.
- `FACT` **Campervan / family-cabin campers.** CamperUpgrade's calculator is aimed at "family dome," "cabin roof pole," "tunnel arch pole," "mixed repair kit" — RV/cabin owners re-cording large aluminium poles. (https://www.camperupgrade.com/tent-pole-shock-cord-length-calculator/)
- `UNKNOWN` Quantitative split by segment (tent owner vs. service operator vs. MYOG) — no market-size study found.

### 1.2 What triggers the purchase

- `FACT` **Cord snapped in the field.** Snuitide: "When the shock cord inside the tent pole has gone slack or snapped, the pole falls apart." (https://snuitide.no/en/gear-repair/replace-tent-pole-shock-cord/)
- `FACT` **Cord gone limp / stretched / "tired."** Kelty: "shock cord that has gotten stretched out, gone limp, snapped, or is just tired and in need of some replacing." (Kelty URL above)
- `FACT` **Loss of elasticity from age.** NEMO: "Shock cords may stretch out over time or snap." (https://www.nemoequipment.com/en-ca/blogs/journal/easy-fix-tent-pole-shock-cord-needs-replacing)
- `FACT` **UV/elemental exposure.** Snuitide attributes failure to "many years of use"; CamperUpgrade attributes it to "exposure to the elements." (both URLs above)
- `FACT` **Pole-tip damage from over-tensioned cord.** The BPL "pole shock cord alternative" thread cites *"This prevents breaks at the ferrels"* as the *reason to replace* shock cord with kite line — i.e., users replace to avoid pole-end fatigue. (https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/59138/)
- `INFERENCE` Trigger is almost always a **symptom on the user's existing gear**, not a proactive "I'm going camping soon" purchase. No brand's listing copy frames shock cord as a pre-trip consumable.

### 1.3 How often does shock cord need replacement?

- `FACT` (SECONDARY via NEMO blog) NEMO: "2–5 years" is the figure the existing Nuotao evidence files cite from NEMO's blog. **Note:** I fetched the NEMO page this session but its body was truncated by the fetcher, so the exact "2–5 years" phrasing is `SECONDARY SOURCE` (carried from `evidence_03_repair_pet_birding.md` §3A) rather than something I re-verified live this round. What I *did* verify live is NEMO's page existence, title, and the "stretch out … or snap" language.
- `FACT` (primary, one voice) BPL user **Sam Farrington**: *"it is no problem replacing the shock cord every couple of years or so at home."* (https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/59138/) — an experienced ultralight user putting the interval at "every couple of years."
- `FACT` (primary, one voice) BPL user **Sam Farrington**, same thread: *"But I've never had shock cord fail on me in the field. Probably because my poles are built to allow easy field repair."* — i.e., for the prepared user, field failure is rare; replacement is a home, elective, periodic event.
- `INFERENCE` The replacement interval is therefore a **2–5 year, home-based, low-frequency event** — not a seasonal consumable. This is the single most important fact for the repeat-purchase question in §7.

### 1.4 Proactive or reactive?

- `FACT` **Reactive.** Every repair article is titled in the problem-first voice: "Easy Fix: Tent Pole Shock Cord Needs Replacing" (NEMO), "How do I repair the shock cord" (Kelty), "How to Fix a Tent Pole" (Sylvan Sport), "How to Replace Shock Cord" (Coghlan's). None is "buy shock cord before your trip."
- `FACT` **Official-brand reactive framing.** Decathlon's entire Forclaz support page is titled "**HAVE YOU BROKEN YOUR TENT? DON'T THROW IT AWAY!**" — a reactive, rescue frame, and it sells shock cord only inside that reactive flow. (https://support.decathlon.co.uk/pole-tent-quickhiker-ultralight-3-persons-forclaz)
- `INFERENCE` The one proactive-adjacent behaviour is the **spare-parts buyer** — Decathlon's tip says *"Remember you can call your store to order parts in advance, and avoid having to make several trips."* This is the *only* proactive purchase behaviour I found, and it is routed to the **manufacturer's own parts desk**, not to a new brand.

### 5.2 The four candidate outcomes, tested against evidence

The question posed: after re-cording a pole, does the customer (A) buy more cord later, (B) buy other tent-repair products, (C) never buy again, or (D) buy from the tent brand's official replacement?

- **(A) Buy more shock cord later — PLAUSIBLE but LONG-LEVERAGED and UNCERTAIN.**
  - `FACT` Supporting: Sam Farrington *"replacing the shock cord every couple of years or so at home"* — this is the **only** direct multi-purchase testimony found.
  - `FACT` Supporting: Snuitide *"if one has gone slack the others are often in the same state"* → a single event can consume the whole 60ft roll and the *next event* reopens a second purchase.
  - `FACT` Weakening: the interval is 2–5 years. A single customer generates at most **~0.4–1.0 purchases per year**, and only for as long as they own a tent they care about repairing.
  - `FACT` Weakening: Farrington's *other* example shows the counter-pattern — he replaced the cord on a used tent he bought and **then sold the tent**. Ownership end = purchase end.
  - `INFERENCE` **(A) is real for a minority of engaged, multi-tent, long-tenure owners — and irrelevant for the majority who own one tent and either never see failure or replace the tent instead.**

- **(B) Buy other tent-repair products — STRUCTURALLY WEAK as a cross-sell from cord.**
  - `FACT` The strongest cross-sell path in all evidence is **manufacturer-led, not cord-led** (Decathlon's single support page bundles shock cord + alu kit + pegs + tape + guyline).
  - `FACT` Users who get ambitious (BPL kite-line poster) cross-sell *away* from shock cord into substitute parts (archery nocks).
  - `INFERENCE` A shock-cord buyer is in a **"single broken part" mindset**, not a kit-buying mindset. Cross-sell works better *out of* a repair-kit purchase *into* shock cord than the reverse.

- **(C) Never buy again — THE DEFAULT OUTCOME, and the largest bucket.**
  - `FACT` Cord lasts 2–5 years; repair is a ~30-minute one-time home job (Snuitide: "a 30-minute job at home"; Kelty: "pretty easily and quickly"). Once done, nothing changes until the next 2–5 year cycle.
  - `FACT` Field snaps are often handled with **free improvised fixes** (tape/paracord — Snuitide), permanently destroying the purchase trigger.
  - `FACT` Many users never experience field failure at all (Farrington: *"I've never had shock cord fail on me in the field"*).
  - `FACT` **Cheap substitutes exist at the point of need:** Coghlan's 18ft ~$4.99, Gear Aid 84" $10.99, and $3 generic cord at any hardware store (Kelty explicitly says "most hardware stores"). A customer with a broken tent at home has 6 cheaper or equal options on day one.
  - `INFERENCE` **(C) is the modal outcome for any *new* brand. The customer's default next behaviour is to buy the cheapest available cord from wherever they already shop, not to remember a brand they bought once 3 years ago for a one-off repair.**

- **(D) Buy from the tent brand's official replacement — REAL AND MATERIAL.**
  - `FACT` Decathlon/Quechua sells official "Tent pole shock cord" as a spare part, with a dedicated repair video, in-store or online, *from the same support page* the owner is already on after a failure. This is a **zero-friction, trust-maximal, context-perfect** capture of the demand.
  - `FACT` Big Agnes sells an official Shock Cord Repair Kit.
  - `FACT` NEMO sends replacements via customer service; Helinox sends spare shock cord/end caps under warranty for free.
  - `FACT` Kelty and Cascade Designs publish the how-to but route buyers to third parties — the one segment where Nuotao is reachable.
  - `INFERENCE` **(D) is the most serious threat to Nuotao.** A customer who just Googled "how do I fix my tent pole" has, on the same page, a branded official spare and a branded how-to video. There is no reason for them to leave that page.

### 5.3 Direct answer to the key question

> *Does the customer come back to Nuotao specifically?*

`INFERENCE` **No — and the evidence points clearly against it.**
1. The purchase event is a **2–5 year, one-off, single-part repair** (§5.2 C).
2. The one user who buys repeatedly (Farrington) buys **by spec (3/32" diameter, Quest Outfitters)** — not by brand loyalty to any cord maker.
3. The strongest repeat-capture mechanisms in the market are **manufacturer-owned** (Decathlon, Big Agnes, NEMO, Helinox) — they own the moment of failure, the trust, and the channel (§5.2 D).
4. **Brand recall for a cord bought once, 3 years ago, for one repair, is effectively zero.** Nothing in the retrieved evidence shows a cord brand ever becoming a habitual destination.
5. `UNKNOWN` I could not retrieve any retailer review containing "bought this again." The absence is itself informative given the commodity pricing ($3 generic vs. $10.99 Gear Aid vs. $30.35 kit).

## 6. Customer Journey Construction

### 6.1 FIRST PURCHASE

- **Trigger:** Existing tent pole's cord snaps or goes limp. Almost always discovered at setup or at a failure, not planned. `FACT` (Kelty, NEMO, Snuitide, CamperUpgrade, all five guides).
- **Behaviour:** Customer searches "how to repair shock cord tent pole" → lands on REI / Sylvan Sport / Kelty / NEMO / Coghlan's / Decathlon / Snuitide how-to content.
- **Decision forks at this moment:**
  - **Fork 1 — has an official-parts brand tent?** → Decathlon/Big Agnes/NEMO/Helinox route them to the **official spare** (Fork D). Highest-conversion for the brand, worst for Nuotao. `FACT`
  - **Fork 2 — tent brand is guide-only (Kelty, Cascade Designs)?** → they're told to buy cord at "hardware stores, REI, Moosejaw, Quest Outfitters." `FACT` (Kelty). **This is Nuotao's only organic entry point.**
  - **Fork 3 — cheap & impatient?** → Coghlan's 18ft $4.99 or $3 hardware-store cord, for a *single* pole. `FACT`
  - **Fork 4 — ambitious / has multiple poles / wants it done once for good?** → the **60ft + lead wire kit** ($16–30). `FACT` — this is Nuotao's format. Snuitide's "check all the poles, they're usually all slack" argument supports this fork. `FACT`
  - **Fork 5 — field snap with no spare?** → duct tape / paracord improvised fix, **no purchase at all.** `FACT` (Snuitide)
- **Product bought:** Either a single short sleeve (84"–18ft) or a 60ft DIY kit.

### 6.2 SECOND PURCHASE

- **When:** 2–5 years later, OR immediately if they repaired one pole of a four-pole tent and the others are also slack. `FACT` (interval: NEMO, Snuitide, Farrington; simultaneity: Snuitide).
- **What / where:** `INFERENCE` Most likely **not Nuotao.** The realistic second-purchase destinations, in order of probability:
  1. Official brand spare (Decathlon/Big Agnes/NEMO) — if they still have that tent. `FACT` channel exists.
  2. Cheapest available cord at wherever they already shop (hardware store, Coghlan's, Gear Aid). `FACT` Kelty explicitly names hardware stores.
  3. A different tent entirely — many users replace the tent rather than repair it repeatedly (Farrington sold his after one repair). `FACT` one voice, but the pattern is visible.
  4. **Nuotao** — only if they (a) remember Nuotao, (b) still own the tent, (c) still prefer Nuotao's format over the official spare. `INFERENCE` All three conditions are simultaneously low-probability.
- **Likely cross-sell in the second window:** `INFERENCE` A tent owner whose cord just failed is 3 years into a gear-lifecycle and is a plausible buyer of **guyline replacement, pole splints, or seam sealant** — i.e., the broader trail-repair kit. This is a *portfolio* cross-sell, not a *same-SKU* repurchase. It matches the existing Nuotao thesis that shock cord is the **#3 attach on a trail-repair-kit funnel**, not a standalone LTV product. `FACT` (US_OUTDOOR_REPEAT_PURCHASE_2026.md §6.3 lists it as attach #3).

### 6.3 THIRD PURCHASE

- `INFERENCE` **Unlikely to be shock cord.** By the third shock-cord event (~6–10 years of tent ownership) the tent is more likely being replaced, the user is a different gear cohort, or the cord has been superseded by an alternative system (kite line + nocks, per the BPL engineer).
- `INFERENCE` Third-purchase value, if any, accrues to the **trail-repair-kit portfolio** (tape, patches, buckles, guyline) rather than to the cord SKU.
- `UNKNOWN` No retrieved evidence of any three-purchase cord customer.

## 7. Key Distinction Analysis: Replacement Frequency ≠ Customer Repeat Purchase

**This is the analytical heart of the research, and it inverts the naive reading.**

| Fact | Why it does NOT create Nuotao repeat revenue |
|---|---|
| `FACT` Shock cord lasts 2–5 years (NEMO; BPL "every couple of years"; Snuitide "many years") | A 2–5 year replacement cycle means **~0.4–1.0 purchases per customer per year**, and only across a multi-year horizon. That is a *low-frequency, long-tail* category — the opposite of a consumable. |
| `FACT` Repair is a one-time, ~30-minute home job (Snuitide; Kelty "pretty easily and quickly") | The purchase is **completed in a single transaction**. Nothing about the fix is consumed repeatedly. |
| `FACT` Field snaps are often solved with free tape/paracord (Snuitide) | **Purchase suppression:** a meaningful share of failure events never becomes a purchase at all. |
| `FACT` Many users never experience field failure (BPL: "I've never had shock cord fail on me in the field") | **Addressable market shrinks further** to owners who actually encounter failure. |
| `FACT` Official-brand spares capture the moment of failure in-channel (Decathlon support page, Big Agnes accessory, NEMO support, Helinox free under warranty) | The highest-intent moment is **already owned by the tent maker**, who is trusted, in-context, and often free. |
| `FACT` The identical 60ft + lead-wire format already sells as a commodity at $16–30 (Amazon.ca B0D7MZC58N $30.35; B091FMVCX4) | Format is **not novel**. Nuotao competes on price/brand against existing unbranded listings. |
| `FACT` The one repeat-buying voice buys **by diameter spec from a specialist (3/32", Quest Outfitters)**, not by cord brand | **Brand loyalty does not exist in this category.** The repeat buyer is spec-driven and channel-driven, not brand-driven. |
| `FACT` $3 generic cord at "most hardware stores" (Kelty's own advice) | The **substitute floor is $3**. Any brand premium must be earned repeatedly. |
| `FACT` Users with ambition *substitute out of the category* (kite line + archery nocks) | The most sophisticated buyers actively **engineer around shock cord**. |

**The asymmetry that matters:**

- **Replacement frequency** answers: "how often does the *world* need shock cord?" → Answer: fairly often, universally, for 2–5 years straight. **This is true and is why the product is real.**
- **Customer repeat purchase** answers: "does *this buyer* come back to *this seller*?" → Answer: **almost never**, because (i) the interval is years, (ii) the moment of need is captured by official brands or $3 substitutes, (iii) no brand loyalty exists in a spec-commodity, (iv) many buyers replace the tent instead, (v) field failures self-extinguish via free fixes.

**`INFERENCE` — Probability a customer returns specifically to Nuotao for shock cord:**

| Outcome | Estimated likelihood | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Never needs to re-cord again (cord still good / tent replaced / failure never occurs) | **~40–55%** | FACT: 2–5yr life, "never had it fail in the field," sold-tent-after-one-repair pattern |
| Needs to re-cord but buys from the **official tent brand** | **~20–30%** | FACT: Decathlon/BigAgnes/NEMO/Helinox all capture in-channel |
| Needs to re-cord but buys the **cheapest generic cord** ($3–$10.99) | **~15–25%** | FACT: $3 hardware-store floor, Coghlan's $4.99, Gear Aid $10.99 |
| **Returns to Nuotao** | **~5–15%** | INFERENCE: requires remembering the brand + still owning the tent + preferring the format, over a 2–5 year gap, in a spec-commodity category with no demonstrated brand loyalty |

> **Note on those percentages:** the buckets are `INFERENCE` — they are my structured reasoning from the evidence above, not measured data. **No vendor published a shock-cord repurchase rate; it does not exist in the market. Treat these as directional bounds, not estimates.** What is defensible is the *ordering*: the official-brand and generic-commodity buckets are demonstrably much larger than the Nuotao bucket.

## 8. Nuotao Verdict

### Verdict: **CONDITIONAL GO** — as an attach/content SKU, NOT as an LTV engine.

### 8.1 What the evidence supports (GO on)

1. `FACT` **The product need is real and multi-source-confirmed.** Six independent sources (NEMO, Kelty, Sylvan Sport, Decathlon, Coghlan's, Snuitide) document the failure mode and the repair. This is not a manufactured need.
2. `FACT` **The 60ft + lead-wire format is commercially validated.** It is already a live, in-production, priced SKU at $16–30 on Amazon. Someone is already making money selling exactly this.
3. `FACT` **The "buy 60ft for one event" economics hold.** Snuitide: if one cord is slack, the other three usually are. One repair event can legitimately consume a full roll. This is the strongest product-design argument for the 60ft roll over an 18ft single kit.
4. `FACT` **Zero regulatory exposure.** Nylon + latex-core cord, solid, no liquid, no hazmat — clearable as general cargo from China. (Consistent with `evidence_03` §3F.)
5. `FACT` **The skill gap is real and content-moatable.** "I have forgotten how to shock cord my poles" (BPL) + CamperUpgrade's ferrule-loss/stretch-window difficulty = a genuine "How to Re-cord a Tent Pole in 20 Minutes" content moat, exactly as the existing plan proposes.
6. `FACT` **Supply chain is proven and cheap** (existing research: Taizhou paracord cluster, ¥0.16–0.80/m). 60ft roll ≈ 200g, small-package friendly.

### 8.2 What the evidence does NOT support (NO-GO on)

1. **Do not build a shock-cord LTV / repeat-purchase thesis.** §7 shows the repurchase path is ~5–15% and structurally undermined by a 2–5yr interval, official-brand capture, and a $3 substitute floor. Any business model requiring cord customers to return is unsound.
2. **Do not position shock cord as the Hero / lead SKU.** The existing Nuotao plan already correctly ranks it **#3** and an *attach* on the trail-repair funnel, not a hero. §6.2 confirms the second purchase is a *portfolio* purchase (guyline, splint, tape), not a cord repurchase. Keep it there.
3. **Do not price against the $3 hardware-store floor on price alone.** You are competing against Coghlan's ($4.99), Gear Aid ($10.99), and generic $3–16 cord. The differentiator has to be **the kit completeness (lead wire + end caps + sizing guide) and the content**, not "we're cheaper."

### 8.3 Conditions attached to the GO

| # | Condition | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Position as a cross-sell/attach, not a standalone LTV SKU.** Do not write a repeat-purchase assumption into the P&L for this SKU. | §5, §7: no evidence for cord-brand repeat purchase. |
| C2 | **Sell the "whole tent, one visit" bundle: 60ft cord + lead wire + end caps (5-pk) + a printed shock-cord length calculator card.** | Snuitide "all four are usually slack"; CamperUpgrade "the length math is the hard part." Both are verified pains that a *card* fixes for $0. This is the real differentiation vs. the $30 generic Amazon kit that ships cord + wire and nothing else. |
| C3 | **Lead with content: "How to Re-cord a Tent Pole in 20 Minutes," including the ferrule-loss and 20–35% stretch-window math.** | The skill gap is verified (BPL "forgotten how"); the math difficulty is verified (CamperUpgrade). This is where Nuotao can beat both Coghlan's and the official brand. |
| C4 | **Target the guide-only-brand owners explicitly (Kelty, Cascade Designs/Big Agnes, Helinox-pre-warranty, NEMO-out-of-warranty, plus all third-party/no-name tents).** | These are the customers the manufacturers *deliberately don't* capture (§3.2 posture (b)). Decathlon/Big Agnes in-warranty owners are not addressable — they're captured. |
| C5 | **Price at $16.99–$22.99** (existing plan), justified by kit completeness + the sizing card, NOT by undercutting. | The $30.35 generic kit already exists; the $4.99 18ft kit already exists. Middle-position on completeness is defensible. |
| C6 | **Validate the repurchase assumption with actual data before any scale spend** — instrument the WooCommerce order event stream for repeat-buyer rate on this SKU over 12 months. Per AGENTS.md §1.2 rule 5 ("禁止凭感觉") and §3.2, the ~5–15% estimate must be *measured*, not assumed, before capital is committed. | No vendor publishes this number; it is unknowable without first-party data. |

### 8.4 One-line verdict

> **Ship it — as the third attach on the trail-repair funnel and as a content-bait SKU. Do not believe, plan, or fund any repeat-purchase story for it.** The cord is a genuinely needed, zero-risk, cheap-to-source product that a customer buys **once every 2–5 years from whoever is cheapest or most trusted at that moment**, and that "whoever" is usually the tent's own manufacturer or a $3 hardware-store roll, not Nuotao.
